The problem

Background noise breaks speech clarity exactly when it matters most.

Calls from a factory floor. Voice commands in a moving vehicle. Hearing aids in a crowded room. Traditional filters remove fixed frequency bands — they don't know what's speech and what isn't.

01

Non-stationary noise

Fans, traffic, and machinery don't sound the same from second to second, so a fixed filter can't keep up.

02

Speech gets lost with the noise

Aggressive filtering that removes background sound often removes parts of the voice too.

03

Needs to run on real hardware

A usable solution has to be light enough to run close to the microphone, not just in a research notebook.

How it works

From noisy waveform to clean audio, in five steps.

The model never touches raw audio directly — it learns in the frequency domain, where speech and noise are easier to tell apart.

01

Capture

A noisy recording comes in as a waveform at 16kHz.

02

STFT

Short-time Fourier transform converts it into a magnitude spectrogram and a phase map.

03

Predict

A neural network trained on paired clean/noisy spectrograms predicts the clean magnitude.

04

Reconstruct

The predicted magnitude is recombined with the original phase.

05

Inverse STFT

The result is converted back into a clean, listenable waveform.
